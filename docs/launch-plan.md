# Launch small this winter, then sell by hand

Road Fund is not ready to take money from strangers yet, but it is close. The software is ready for a hand-held pilot. What is missing is the business around it. Before the first real bill you need a company (an LLC, a federal tax ID and a business bank account), live Stripe and email running on a real domain, published terms and privacy pages, your own name, phone number and mailing address on the About page, and a way to find out the moment a road signs up. Two money decisions also come first. With Road Fund's current Stripe setup, **Road Fund, not the road, covers any card chargeback or bounced bank payment**. Stripe's platform charges ($2 per road in any month it gets paid out, plus 0.25% and 25¢ per payout) can also cost more than the $1 per payment on larger bills. Getting through all of this takes **about 30–50 hours of evenings, roughly three to five weeks at your pace**. It costs roughly $150–$1,100 in fees, plus a $500–$1,000 cushion you leave in Stripe and get back later, plus an estimated $500–$2,000 if you buy the recommended lawyer and accountant hours. Approval for texting can take weeks, so launch on email and paper. Plowing contracts for this winter were settled by October, so the plan is: **pilot with three to five roads you know in December–February, launch publicly for spring grading in March 2027 with a target of 10 paying roads by June, and make the big push in June–October 2027**, when captains line up plow contracts. Paying customers who renew will come from setting up each road on the phone with its captain, a free first season for founding roads, the road paying (about $5–$15 per home) instead of the captain, and renewals timed before billing season. A big free funnel won't get you there: at a typical 3–5% free-to-paid rate, 100 paying roads would take about 2,000 free ones. Do your outreach in this order: your own network, then those roads' contractors and neighbors, then Maine's road-association community, then lake associations, and only later title companies and paid ads. Never cold-call or cold-text anyone. Expect 100 paying roads in late 2027 or 2028, worth $11,000–$18,000 a year. That proves the business works. It is not yet a salary.

## The software is ready for a pilot; the business around it is not

Everything a captain or neighbor touches is built and tested (from the code): the setup wizard, the captain app, the neighbor pay page, Stripe payments with a $1 Road Fund fee on each, emails through Resend, texts through Twilio with STOP handling, the daily reminder job, sign-in by emailed link, an About page and a sample road. The changes from the last design round are in the code. The code also lets only the neighbor turn on texts from their own page. That matters, because carriers reject texting programs that lack clear consent from the person being texted ([Twilio error 30884](https://live.docs.dev.twilio.com/docs/api/errors/30884.md)), and a captain typing in a neighbor's number doesn't count as the neighbor agreeing. The gaps are not in the product. They are in four other places: legal footing, money risk, texting approval, and whether you can even see what is happening.

| Area | Where it stands | Must be done before |
|---|---|---|
| Captain app, wizard, pay page, reminders, sign-in, sample road | Built and tested | Already done |
| Company: LLC, federal tax ID (EIN), business bank account | Missing | The first real payment |
| Live Stripe keys and webhook, Upstash Redis on Vercel, domain name, site address, cron password (`CRON_SECRET`) | Setup work, not new code | The first real payment |
| Email sending domain (Resend, plus the SPF, DKIM and DMARC records) | Missing | The first bill |
| Your name, phone, town and mailing address on the About page | Blank (it reads them from settings) | The first real captain |

| An email to you when a road signs up, and one list of all roads | Missing | The pilot |
| Error alerts, an "is the site up" alert, your own nightly backup, signup counts | Missing | The pilot |
| Texts (a Twilio number and carrier registration) | Missing. Approval takes 1–6 weeks | Turning texts on, not launch day |
| The $249 "help with the agreement" service | A manual service with legal risk | Hold it (see below) |
| Business insurance | None | The public launch in March |

The missing alert matters more than it looks. Your sales method is a phone call within a day of signup. With no alert and no list of roads, you won't know a road exists until its captain writes to you. Treat the new-road email and the roads list as sales tools.

The blank About page is a launch blocker too, not a detail. Older adults' reported fraud losses rose from about $600 million in 2020 to **$2.4 billion in 2024** ([FTC, via search excerpt](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P144400-OlderAdultsReportDec2025.pdf)). Long-standing web-credibility guidance says the simplest proof of a real business is a real person, a street or P.O. box address, and easy contact ([Stanford Web Credibility Guidelines](https://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines)). Only you can supply those facts.

### Stripe makes Road Fund cover every reversed payment

Road Fund uses Stripe's "Express" accounts with the money routed through the platform ("destination charges"). Under that setup, Stripe's documentation says **the platform is responsible when a road's account goes negative**, whatever the account settings ([Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/docs/connect/account-balances)). For card disputes, Stripe takes the disputed amount and the dispute fee from the platform ([Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/disputes)). Bank payments carry a longer tail. A consumer can generally dispute a bank debit for **up to 60 calendar days** ([Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/link/instant-bank-payments/disputed-payments)), and these returns usually can't be challenged ([Adyen](https://docs.adyen.com/risk-management/chargeback-guidelines/ach-chargebacks)). Here is what that means for you. A neighbor pays $400 from checking. The captain is paid out. Six weeks later the neighbor's bank reverses it. Stripe takes the $400 from Road Fund, which is about 400 payments' worth of your $1 fees, unless you can pull it back from the captain.

Stripe's platform fees are the second issue. When the platform sets the prices, as Road Fund does now, Stripe charges **$2 per account in each month it receives a payout, plus 0.25% and 25¢ per payout** ([Stripe Support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/monetizing-payments-with-stripe-connect); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/software/learn/stripe-fees)). The code passes Stripe's card and bank processing fees through to the road. It does not set a payout schedule, so Stripe's default schedule applies. The arithmetic below (mine, from Stripe's published fees) covers one bill a year in which every home pays online. The "worst case" column assumes each payment is paid out separately over two months.

| Road and bill | Your $1 fees | Stripe platform charges, worst case | Stripe platform charges, one payout a month |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 homes × $200 = $1,000 | $5 | $7.75 | $4.75 |
| 10 homes × $400 = $4,000 | $10 | $16.50 | $12.25 |
| 30 homes × $400 = $12,000 | $30 | $41.50 | $32.25 |

The 0.25% charge alone equals $1 on a $400 payment. So on bigger shares, the $1 fee loses a little money even with one payout a month. These are dollars per road per year. A paying road ($79–$149) easily covers them, and a free road costs a few dollars. Other charges sit on top, so check that Stripe's per-check fee for instant bank verification, listed at $1.50 in earlier research, is part of what you pass through ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/pricing)). Tax forms are a smaller issue than they first look. When the platform sets prices, the platform must file 1099-K forms ([Stripe Support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/who-is-responsible-for-1099-tax-reporting-after-migrating-standard-connected-accounts)). But the federal threshold is back to **more than $20,000 and more than 200 payments a year** ([IRS](https://irs.gov/newsroom/irs-issues-faqs-on-form-1099-k-threshold-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-dollar-limit-reverts-to-20000)), so almost no road will need one. Filing runs $2.99 per federal form and $1.49 per state ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/en-si/connect/1099)). Some states set lower thresholds ([Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/1099-K)), which is one more question for the accountant.

There is a cheap way out of most of this. In June 2026 Stripe released a preview that lets Express accounts use Stripe's own pricing and makes **Stripe, not the platform, responsible for losses** ([Stripe changelog](https://docs.stripe.com/changelog/dahlia/2026-06-24/express-connected-accounts-v2-stripe-owned-pricing-negative-balance-liability)). No source confirms that it works with Road Fund's payment routing, so ask Stripe in writing before launch. If Stripe says yes, the platform fees, the loss risk and the tax filing all move off Road Fund. If it says no, protect yourself four ways. Pay roads out weekly or monthly, with a delay, so bank payments clear first and you pay fewer per-payout fees. Turn on Stripe's setting that lets it pull a shortfall back from the captain's bank account (called "debit negative balances"). Keep $500–$1,000 of your own money in Stripe as a cushion. And either add the 0.25% to the fee the road already covers, or accept the small loss. Switching to "direct charges" (each road's own account becomes the merchant) would also shift fees and losses away from you, but it means a larger code change.

The legal picture for moving money is favorable as long as you keep the current design. Stripe holds the state money-transmitter licenses ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/de-dk/spc/licenses)). Federal rules exempt payment processors that settle only through regulated banking channels ([FinCEN FIN-2014-R009](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/administrative_ruling/FIN-2014-R009.pdf)). And Road Fund never holds a road's money. Keep it that way: never park road money in Road Fund's account, never pay a contractor out of a pool Road Fund holds, and never move money between roads. No Stripe page states the US position outright, so spend one hour with a payments lawyer before the first real payment. When you set up Stripe, describe the business plainly ("collecting shared private-road maintenance costs from neighboring homeowners"). Stay away from words like "bill pay," "crowdfunding" or "money transfer," which sit near Stripe's restricted list ([Stripe Support](https://support.stripe.com/embedded-connect/questions/restricted-business-list-faqs)). The code already never adds card fees to a neighbor's payment. That keeps you clear of card-network surcharge rules and state surcharge bans ([Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/03/insights-finance-ftec-want-to-have-customers-cover-payment)). If a road chooses to pass the bank fee on, that fee must be shown before the neighbor pays. Ask the lawyer about state "junk fee" rules too.

### Texts wait on carrier approval, so launch on email and paper

Texting is the slowest item and the most likely to stall launch. Twilio's rules for platforms say each customer should have its own sender registration and that platforms must not share one across customers ([Twilio Docs](https://www.twilio.com/docs/messaging/compliance/a2p-10dlc/onboarding-isv)). For a captain without a tax ID, that means a sole-proprietor registration in the captain's name, verified from the captain's own cell phone, at **$4 plus $15 one-time and about $2 a month** per road ([Twilio](https://pages.twilio.com/10DLC-HelpArticles-WW)). The alternative is to register Road Fund's LLC once, as the sender of its own account notices, either on a regular number or on a toll-free number. Toll-free verification has required a business tax ID since February 2026 ([Twilio changelog](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/changelog/business-registration-numbers-required-for-toll-free-messaging-p)). Ask Twilio support in writing which model applies, and budget for the per-road one just in case. Twilio quotes up to 5 business days, but carrier vetting can take 5–30 business days ([Twilio Support](https://support.twilio.com/hc/en-us/articles/11587910480155-A2P-10DLC-Campaign-Vetting-FAQ)). Rejections often come from the privacy policy. It must say, in so many words, that **mobile opt-in data will not be shared with third parties or affiliates for marketing or promotional purposes** ([Twilio error 30919](https://www.twilio.com/docs/api/errors/30919)). Put that sentence in before you apply.

Email is quick but has its own rules. Verify a sending subdomain in Resend, then add a DMARC record. Gmail started rejecting mail from senders that don't meet its rules in November 2025 ([Zoho community, via search](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/community/topic/gmail-is-ramping-up-its-email-sender-policy-from-november-2025)). Resend's free plan allows **3,000 emails a month but only 100 a day** ([Automation Atlas](https://automationatlas.io/answers/resend-free-tier-explained-2026/)), so move to a paid plan before one day's reminders for many roads can hit that cap. Starting on email and paper costs you little. The captain's printed letter to neighbors should come first anyway, and older neighbors are used to paper.

### Sell the letter and the template now, hold the $249 service

The agreement template can launch if it is clearly a fill-in form with a prominent "not a law firm, not a substitute for an attorney" notice. Put the notice on the page and on the first page of every document it produces. Texas wrote that kind of software into its law as not being the practice of law, on condition that the disclaimer is clear ([Tex. Gov't Code 81.101](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/government-code/gov-t-sect-81-101/)). The $249 "help with the agreement" service is riskier. State ethics opinions keep finding it improper when a non-lawyer service keeps part of a lawyer's fee, even if it collects from the client first ([NYSBA Op. 1271](https://nysba.org/app/uploads/2024/08/Opn-1271-with-letterhead.pdf); [Ohio Op. 2016-3](https://ezel.ai/ethics-opinions/ohbpc/2016-003-lawyer-participation-in-referral-services)). The safer version works like this. The road hires and pays the lawyer directly. Road Fund charges a flat fee only for its own non-legal work, such as gathering road facts, packaging the draft and scheduling. Road Fund takes nothing from lawyers. It is also hand work you would do yourself on weeknights. Take it off sale until a one-hour ethics consult approves the structure and you have the hours to deliver it.

The $150 Road Status Letter can launch. A fair, fixed fee for a document you actually produce fits the federal real-estate settlement law (RESPA), which bans kickbacks for referrals but allows payment for services actually performed ([CFPB Regulation X](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1024/14/)). $150 sits inside the typical $150–$500 range for HOA resale packages ([LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/how-do-i-order-an-hoa-resale-package/)). The rule to live by: **never give title officers, real estate agents, loan officers or closing attorneys money, gifts, credits or free service for sending letter orders**. Federal guidance says flat fees don't fix a referral payment ([HUD 2010 rule](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2010-06-25/pdf/2010-15355.pdf)), and state attorneys general are still bringing these cases ([National Mortgage News](https://www.nationalmortgagenews.com/news/title-company-settles-respa-jv-referral-claims-for-1m)). If you credit part of the $150 to the road's fund, ask the lawyer first. Before selling letters in a state, check whether it caps fees for homeowner-association resale papers, because a road association could count as one. On sales tax, about 25 states and jurisdictions tax software subscriptions ([Anrok](https://www.anrok.com/resources/sales-tax-statistics)). Other states only matter once you pass about $100,000 or 200 sales there ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/en-gb-gi/guides/introduction-to-us-sales-tax-and-economic-nexus)). Only your home state matters this year.

## The 2026 plowing window has closed, so pilot now and sell in spring

Snow contractors say the best time to sign a contract is summer. Contracts are set in late summer and early fall, routes fill by October ([Ruppert Landscape](https://www.ruppertlandscape.com/timing-everything-contract-snow-ice-removal-services-summer/); [Fresh Cut Professionals](https://freshcutprofessionals.com/blog/2024/11/27/residential-snow-removal-reliable/)), and some neighbor plow contracts are due and paid by October 1 or October 31 ([St. Paul](https://www.stpaul.gov/sites/default/files/2023-11/Alley%20Berkeley%20St%20Clair%20Snelling%20Macalester%20Plowing%20Contract.Deb_.pdf); [Town of Ashland](https://www.townofashlandwi.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/SNOW-REMOVAL-CONTRACT.docx)). ## Recurring customers come from hand-held setups, not a big free funnel

Every comparable product started close to its first users and grew outward from there. Tithe.ly launched at the founder's own church ([Tithe.ly](https://get.tithe.ly/blog/how-did-tithe-ly-get-started)). PayHOA's own case studies show one board referred by its banker, another choosing from online reviews, and a third buyer who was closed by **a direct conversation with the founder** ([PayHOA case study](https://www.payhoa.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/PayHOA-CaseStudy-Your-HOA-Community-Management.pdf); [PayHOA West Springs](https://www.payhoa.com/case-study-west-springs)). A study of fast-growing business-software companies found personal networks were the most common source of the first ten customers ([Lenny's Newsletter, via summaries](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-win-your-first-10-b2b-customers)). TeamSnap spread because a coach would hear about it, sign up and invite the whole team ([Latka, 2020](https://getlatka.com/companies/teamsnap/interviews/teamsnap-dave-dupont-2020)). That loop is weak for Road Fund: parents belong to many teams, but neighbors usually live on one road. Plan on captains telling captains, and contractors telling captains, not on neighbors turning into captains.

The arithmetic points the same way. Self-serve free plans convert about **3–5% to paid at a "good" level and 6–8% at a "great" one, and products with sales help reach 10–15%** ([Lenny's Newsletter, via summaries; contested](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-a-good-free-to-paid-conversion)). At 5%, 100 paying roads would need about 2,000 free roads, which this niche won't produce. If 25–40% of roads you set up by phone go on to pay (a planning assumption, not a benchmark), you need 250–400 captains to start. So the business model is a few roads converted by hand, not a big free funnel.

### Set up each road on the phone

Stripe's founders had a rule: when someone was interested, they didn't send a link; they said "give me your laptop" and set the person up on the spot ([alexanderjarvis.com](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/stripe-doing-things-that-dont-scale)). Road Fund's version is a 30–45 minute phone or video call. You enter the homes, the split and the first bill while the captain watches, then send a test bill to the captain's own phone. That goes straight at what slows rural older adults down. A 2024 study found that knowledge, perceived value, confidence and social support drive their technology adoption ([Chan et al., 2024](https://doaj.org/article/0d38f6aba7904e09821b586e5e06fe69)). Research on new ideas finds that complexity is the one trait that slows adoption ([Aston University review](https://research.aston.ac.uk/en/publications/innovation-adoption-attributes-a-review-and-synthesis-of-research)). Budget 45–60 minutes per road including prep and follow-up, so ten roads take about one week of your hours. That figure is an estimate. Time your first five setups and replace it with real numbers. Mail a handwritten thank-you note afterward. Wufoo did it for its customers, and it fits this audience ([CustomerThink](https://customerthink.com/mitchells_and_wufoo_hug_their_customers_with_handwritten_notes/)).

Older neighbors will worry about money and trust more than about which buttons to press. About **two-thirds of adults 50+ who don't use payment apps say a major reason is that they don't trust them with their money** ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2022/09/08/payment-apps-like-venmo-and-cash-app-bring-convenience-and-security-concerns-to-some-users/)). Check writing rises steeply with age ([Atlanta Fed, via search excerpt](https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/publications/policy-hub-macroblog/2026/10/08/surveying-consumers-use-of-personal-checks)). In 2017, after **74% of churches offered online giving**, it still made up only about 15% of giving ([The Alabama Baptist](https://thealabamabaptist.org/?p=59146)). So sell online payment as "another way to pay," never "the new way." On a 10-home road, two or three check payers is normal. Rural residents are also more likely than city residents to know most of their neighbors: 40% against 24% ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2018/05/22/how-urban-suburban-and-rural-residents-interact-with-their-neighbors/)). And recommendations from people you know are the most trusted kind of advertising, at 89% ([Nielsen](https://develop.nielsen.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2022/02/2021-Nielsen-Trust-In-Advertising-U.S.-sell-sheet.pdf)). So the captain does the selling to neighbors. Your job is to make the captain look credible.

### Offers that lower the risk without lowering the price

| Offer | Exact form | Why | How strong the evidence is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free first season for founding pilot roads | Free until August 1, 2027, in exchange for a weekly 15-minute call and an honest written comment | Value shows only after one collection | Inference. Nobody has tested it with volunteer buyers |
| Founding price lock | "$79 or $149 a year, locked for as long as you stay" for the first roads in each county | A reason to act now without discounting. Bootstrapped founders most often price too low ([Niche Pursuits](https://www.nichepursuits.com/?p=142629)) | Untested |
| A plain, full-season guarantee | "If your first paid bill doesn't come in easier than last year, call me and I'll refund the year. No forms." No asterisk | Longer guarantees cut perceived risk more ([Behe & Fry](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/jriskr/v23y2020i2p242-258.html)), most for things you can only judge by using them ([Chen & Hou](https://dba.nkust.edu.tw/uploads/asset/data/6232a03a2e6356240a2dd412/28.pdf)). One test showed +21% sales and 12% refunds ([Quick Sprout](https://www.quicksprout.com/what-converts-better-free-trial-versus-money-back-guarantee/)). Fine print ruins trust ([Abmatic](https://abmatic.ai/blog/how-to-use-guarantees-to-increase-conversions-on-landing-page-for-saas)) | Moderate. The test wasn't for software |
| The road pays, not the captain | Show the plan as a road expense: $79 ÷ 8 homes ≈ $10, $149 ÷ 30 homes ≈ $5, about $15 a home on a 10-home road | Turns a personal cost into a shared one, the way TeamSnap has the team pay ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/teamsnap/interviews/teamsnap-dave-dupont-2020)) | Adjacent evidence only |
| A season free for both roads when a captain refers another road | Offered after a captain's first successful collection | Captain-to-captain is the strongest loop available | Untested. Allowed because it rewards the subscription, not letters |

Bill yearly only, and don't add monthly plans. One source puts 12-month retention at **92% for annual plans against 68% for monthly ones** ([Baremetrics](https://baremetrics.com/blog/annual-vs-monthly-pricing-better-retention)). But a yearly renewal is a high-risk moment that needs a plan ([Recurly](https://www.recurly.com/research/churn-rate-benchmarks)), and failed cards cause an estimated **20–50% of all cancellations** ([ChargeHive](https://www.chargehive.com/blog/involuntary-churn-vs-voluntary-churn)). Send notices 30 and 7 days before renewal with a year summary ("you collected $X from Y homes"). Offer bank payment for the subscription too. Move every renewal to August, so captains renew before fall billing, not in the middle of it.

Watch the free plan closely. If one bill a year is all most roads need, few will ever pay. The natural reason to upgrade is the second bill: winter plowing plus spring grading. Other reasons are reminders, a vote or the agreement. Downgrading must never delete history, so "we didn't need it this year" becomes a downgrade, not a lost road. Handing the road to the next captain is the other reason roads stay. Treasurer-handoff guides name missing records and an unreachable former treasurer as the hard cases ([MoneyMinder](https://moneyminder.com/resources/guide-to-a-smooth-treasurer-transition/)). "All the road's history is here" is a reason to keep paying.

The money per road is small, which limits what you can spend to win each one. A 60/40 mix of $79 and $149 roads averages about $107 a year. Add letters from an assumed sale every two to three years on a 10-home road, and each paying road brings in roughly **$110–$180 a year**. At 25–40% of roads leaving each year, that is about **$275–$720 over a road's life**, so a road is worth winning only if it costs well under $100–$240. That rules out paid search, trade shows and long sales calls. It allows about **1–2 hours of your time per paying road**. A hundred roads come to about **$11,000–$18,000 a year** (all arithmetic from the go-to-market notes). Hold your prices. There is no room to cut them.

Electric co-op magazines are tempting. Readers say they read most issues: in Texas, 90% say they always or usually read each copy ([Texas Electric Cooperatives](https://texas-ec.org/?p=8064)). But that comes from the magazines' own surveys, ad rates are only available on request, and most readers don't run a road. Every Door Direct Mail costs about 26¢ a piece in postage ([4over4](https://v2.4over4.com/guide/eddm-pricing-explained)). It only makes sense for a mail route that is mostly camp or lake roads. Reddit expects nine helpful posts for every promotional one ([Redship](https://redship.io/blog/reddit-self-promotion-rules)). Use it to learn how people describe the problem in their own words, not to find leads.

Start with Maine plus wherever you live. Maine is the only state with an organized road-association community, a state guide to forming road associations, and a cheap list of named associations. Your home area is where your network, your local knowledge and your sales-tax state already are. Add Vermont, New Hampshire, Wisconsin, Michigan and Minnesota through lake associations in the spring. Earlier research found that Vermont, New Hampshire, Wisconsin, Pennsylvania and Maine have default laws that make neighbors share road costs, which gives you an honest opening line ("your state's law already says neighbors share the cost"). Michigan and Minnesota have no such law but have large lake-association networks.

Three rules keep the outreach safe. First, **never cold-call or cold-text a captain**. Among older adults who lost $10,000 or more to impersonation scams in 2024, 41% were first contacted by phone ([FTC, via search excerpt](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Imposter-Data-Spotlight-August-2025.pdf)). Marketing texts without consent can cost $500–$1,500 per message under federal law ([Shumaker](https://shumakerwilliams.com/business-to-business-sms-marketing-navigating-can-spam-and-tcpa-regulations/)). Cold email is legal if it carries your postal address and an opt-out. It gets about 0.1% response on cold lists ([directmail.io](https://directmail.io/direct-mail-response-rate)), so use it only to follow up with people who wrote or called. Second, never pay or gift anyone in the real-estate chain for letter orders. Free seminars open to every agent are fine ([RealTrends on CFPB FAQs](https://www.realtrends.com/blog/2020/12/08/respa-guidance-on-gifts-and-promotions/)). Realtors must disclose any benefit they receive for recommending a service ([NAR Code](https://www.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/policies/2017/2017-COE-Poster.pdf)), so the clean path is to pay them nothing. Third, rewards to captains or contractors for bringing in roads are allowed. Anyone who mentions the reward publicly must disclose it ([Prefinery](https://www.prefinery.com/blog/ftc-endorsement-guides-for-referral-programs/)). Testimonials follow the FTC's 2024 reviews rule. No reward may depend on a positive comment. Any free service must be disclosed next to the quote. Friends and relatives count as insiders ([Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2024/11/ftc-faqs-on-consumer-review-testimonials-rule); [BCLP](https://bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/part-3-what-the-ftcs-final-rule-means-for-incentivized-reviews.html)). The FTC sent warning letters under that rule in December 2025 ([Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/ftc-warns-companies-of-potential-violations-of-the-consumer-review-rule)).

## Scripts and handouts you can use this winter

These are drafts built from the research patterns, not tested copy. Replace every bracket with real facts. Use "I live on a private road" only if it is true.

**Interview questions (ask about the past, never pitch).** Opinions about a future product are unreliable. The goal is a commitment of time, reputation or money ([Sachin Rekhi on The Mom Test](https://www.sachinrekhi.com/the-mom-test-rob-fitzpatrick)).

> "How did you collect for plowing last year?" · "Who didn't pay, and what did you do about it?" · "Who fronted the money, and for how long?" · "Where is the road agreement kept, if there is one?" · "What happened the last time a house on the road sold?" · "When do you get the plow and gravel quotes?"
>
> Never ask "Would you use an app for this?" End with one concrete ask: "Would you send this season's bill through it with me on the phone?", "Could you introduce me to your plow contractor?", or "Would the road pay $79 for the year?"

**First-contact letter** (one page in a plain #10 envelope, signed, with your photo):

> I'm [Name]. I [live on a private road in ___ County / built this after ___]. I made a simple way for the person who pays the road bills to get paid back by the neighbors. It sends each house its share by text, email or paper, records checks too, and keeps the road agreement and a letter for when a house sells. It's free for one bill a year. If you'd like help setting it up, call me at [number] and I'll do it with you on the phone. I'll never call you out of the blue or ask for bank details. You can see a sample road at [your domain]/[sample]. — [Name], [mailing address], [website]

**Founding pilot road offer:**

> You'd be one of the first five roads. It's free until August 1, 2027. I'll set it up with you on the phone and send your first bill with you watching. In return, I'd ask for a 15-minute call each week through the winter and your honest opinion, good or bad. After that it's $79 a year (up to 8 homes) or $149 (9 to 30), locked at that price for as long as you stay, and the road can pay it from dues.

**The captain's letter to neighbors** (sent before the first bill, so the first text is never a surprise):

> Neighbors — For the last few years I've paid the gravel and plow bills and then collected from each of you. This year I'm trying a simpler way. You'll get an email or text from me, through a service called Road Fund, showing what each house owes and what it's for. You can pay from that link, or mail me a check like always. The money goes to [the road's account / my bank account], and everyone can see the same list of who has paid. Nobody will ever call asking for your bank information. Questions? Call me at ___. — [Captain's full name], [road name]

**Meeting handout (half a page):** last year's costs, this year's estimate, each house's share, the ways to pay (link, check, cash to the captain), the Road Fund cost per home if the road pays it, and a line for anyone who wants phone help. Churches and co-ops that moved older members to online payment kept paper options and offered phone help ([Piedmont EMC](https://pemc.coop/paperless-billing/)).

**Referral ask (after a captain's first good collection):** "Glad it went smoothly. Do you know another road captain, or does your plow contractor plow other roads? If one of them sets up, you both get a season free."

**Testimonial request:** "Would you be willing to tell me, in your own words, how the first bill went, good or bad? I may share what you write on the website with your first name and county if you say that's OK. It won't change your price either way." If the road got free service, the quote says so: "Pat's road got its first season free as a founding road."

**Contractor flyer line:** "Plow private roads? Get paid once by the whole road instead of chasing each house. Road Fund collects each neighbor's share before the work and keeps a record of what the road approved. Free for the road's first bill. Questions: [your name and number]."

**Title company and agent one-pager line:** "When a file has a private road, Road Fund can produce one $150 letter showing the agreement status, dues owed and the road balance, paid at closing. If the road has no written agreement, the seller's neighbors can get one drafted and signed. Start early, because collecting every signature takes weeks." Add a seller checklist whose first step reads: "If your road has no written agreement, start one now." Agreements take "a few weeks to several months" to put in place ([Bridge Legal](https://bridgelegal.org/private-road-maintenance-agreement-how-obtain-use/)).

**The question to send Stripe (in writing):** "Can our Express connected accounts use `fees_collector=stripe` and `losses_collector=stripe` with destination charges that use `on_behalf_of`, or only with direct charges?"

**The question to send Twilio (in writing):** "We're a platform that sends account notices (bills, receipts, reminders) to neighbors on private roads on behalf of volunteer road captains, most of whom have no tax ID. Should Road Fund LLC register one brand and campaign (or a toll-free number) for its own account notifications, or does each road need its own secondary profile and sole-proprietor brand?"

## Conclusion

The software is not the hard part. The day Road Fund takes a stranger's money, it becomes a small payments business. Under its current Stripe setup it carries the loss when a payment is reversed, and its trust rests on one named person with a phone. That person is you, and your scarce resource is about ten hours a week. Two things follow. First, the cheapest risk fixes are questions, not code: one written question to Stripe could remove the loss risk, the platform fees and the tax filing at once, and one to Twilio settles how texting gets approved. Second, you should judge every channel by captains reached per hour of your time, not by audience size. That is why letters to named road associations and introductions from contractors beat co-op magazines and Facebook. On this ten-hour budget the plowing calendar is unforgiving: miss a summer and you lose a year. So the date that matters most is August 2027, when you want 10 or more paying roads, honest quotes and a few friendly contractors in hand.

The biggest open question is not about channels. It is whether a typical road needs more than one bill a year. If most roads don't, the free plan is the whole product for them. Revenue will then depend on the second bill (winter plowing plus spring grading), the letter at a home sale, and the move from a handshake to a written agreement. Ask that question in every interview this winter. The answer decides whether 100 roads is a business that can keep growing or a hobby that pays its own costs.
