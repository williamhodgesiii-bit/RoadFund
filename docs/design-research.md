# Trade template polish for checkable proof

Road Fund looks AI-built because of how its pages are put together. Its colors and fonts are not the cause. The landing page stacks the patterns that 2025–2026 designers list as "vibe-coded" tells: a pill badge above the headline, an HTML-drawn phone with tilted floating notification cards, three- and four-card grids with icons in tinted squares, numbered step cards, and same-radius bands one after another. The copy adds forced threes, an "X, not Y" contrast and "Built so…" claims. A 2026 audit of about 1,590 Show HN pages found 22% carrying four or more of these patterns. The pine-and-paper palette and the Atkinson Hyperlegible and Overpass fonts appear on no tell list, so they should stay. What makes a small product look established is a set of facts people can check: a named founder, a town, a phone number and a mailing address, prices with every fee in dollars, a full sample of the letter it sells, real screenshots and real road photos. The owner has to supply all of these, and none of them may be invented. A money product is judged harder than most. In Stanford's 2,684-person credibility study, "design look" drew 46.1% of all comments, rising to 54.6% on finance sites. On the flow side, adults 65+ finished about 55% of web tasks against 75% for younger adults and gave up almost twice as often. The captain app should therefore open on a to-do list, keep every control large and labeled, and show a full review page before anything sends money or messages. The neighbor pay page has to look like the opposite of a scam text: who sent it, the amount, why, one action, three visible ways to pay with fees in dollars, and a person to call. The first changes are the identity details, a plain hero with a real screenshot, a task-list home screen and a server-rendered pay page. Most sources were read through search summaries because the primary pages were blocked. The AI-tell lists reflect designer consensus and blog-level audits, not tests with older rural users, and weaker claims are flagged where they appear.

## Six layout habits make Road Fund look AI-made, and the palette is not one of them

In 2026 people started measuring the "AI-made" look instead of just complaining about it. Software engineer Adrian Krebs ran about **1,590 Show HN landing pages** through a headless browser, with one test per pattern: Inter in a centered headline, purple color schemes, a badge above the heading, cards with a colored edge, numbered steps, rows of statistics, frosted glass. He found **22% "heavy" (four or more patterns), 32% "mild" and 46% "clean"** ([Adrian Krebs](https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/)). He put his false-positive rate at 5–10% and said a single pattern proves nothing. A critic noted that the thresholds were tuned on 202 of the same sites ([GIGAZINE summary](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260423-design-slop/)). A self-published audit of 120 pages built with Lovable, Bolt, Replit, Claude Code and Cursor found that only 20 showed two or fewer of its 16 habits. It reported icon-topped feature cards on **63%** of pages and untouched shadcn colors on 61%, though those figures are the author's own ([Build to Launch](https://buildtolaunch.substack.com/p/fix-ai-slop-website-design)). Named checklists cover the rest: a colored stripe down one side of every card, borders around every block, one colored or italic-serif accent word in the headline, and all-caps eyebrow labels over each section ([Paralect](https://www.paralect.com/blog/signs-of-ai-slop-in-design)), plus "the same rounded corners and the same soft shadow on every box" ([vibecodekit](https://vibecodekit.dev/ai-slop-design)). The cause is mundane. AI coding tools default to React, Tailwind and shadcn, whose examples used `indigo-500`, so their output converges on "the statistical average of every interface it was trained on" ([DEV](https://dev.to/alanwest/why-every-ai-built-website-looks-the-same-blame-tailwinds-indigo-500-3h2p); [Rottoways](https://rottoways.com/blog/ai-generated-website-looks-generic)). One designer defines slop as "design with no clear author" ([Kosta Canatselis](https://world.hey.com/kostac/spot-the-slop-a-ui-designer-s-guide-to-fixing-ai-defaults-4c448c9c)). The detection guides add that the tells say more about a design nobody reviewed closely than about who wrote the code ([vibe0](https://www.vibe0.com.au/blog/how-to-tell-if-a-website-is-vibe-coded)).

Measured against those lists, Road Fund's landing page (`app/(site)/page.tsx`) shows at least six of the named patterns. The headline already sits left-aligned beside the phone, which avoids the centered-hero tell, but most of what surrounds it matches. No source named Lucide icons, FAQ accordions or HTML phone mockups specifically. Those three mappings are inferences from the broader "fake product UI" and "icons in tinted squares" tells.

| Pattern on the tell lists | Where Road Fund has it | Replace it with |
|---|---|---|
| Badge above the main headline | Yellow `sign-chip` reading "For private roads shared by 3 to 30 homes" above the H1; a "Most roads" chip on the plan card (`components/plan-cards.tsx`) | The audience stated in plain text in the headline or subhead. Drop "Most roads": with no sign-up data behind it, it is also an unsupported claim |
| Fake product UI | `components/phone-mock.tsx`: a phone drawn in HTML, with two tilted floating cards ("Road account $2,093.53", "Becker paid $202.25") | A real screenshot of the neighbor page and the real text message, at true size, cropped to the amount and the Pay button |
| Identical icon-topped cards; icons in small tinted squares | Three problem cards; four "Built so your neighbors trust it" cards; tinted icon chips in the agreement list and the snow band | A paragraph or plain rows. Icons appear only inline, next to the word they label |
| Numbered 1-2-3 step cards | "How it works" as three numbered cards with icons | A worked bill for a labeled sample road |
| Same radius, shadow and padding on every box; borders around everything | Every section is a rounded card or a full-width band, alternating paper and white | Borderless sections separated by space and thin rules. Borders only on the payment box, documents and status messages |
| All-caps eyebrow labels | "Road Status Letter" over the sample letter, "Next up" on the captain home, the road name over the neighbor greeting | Sentence-case headings. Keep a small label only when it carries a date ("Vote by Dec 12") |
| A closing band that repeats the hero | Pine band: "Get this year's road money in without the chasing" | End on contact details and one plain button |

The palette and fonts can stay. No source lists pine, paper, road-sign yellow, brick or lake blue, and none lists Atkinson Hyperlegible or Overpass. The font tells are specific families: "Inter is the #1 slop tell. Inter is excellent, which is exactly the problem" ([AI Skill Market](https://aiskill.market/blog/banning-inter-the-font-tell)), followed by Geist, Space Grotesk and Instrument Serif with an italic accent word ([Developers Digest](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/ai-design-slop-and-how-to-spot-it)). Road Fund's pairing has a story worth telling. Atkinson Hyperlegible was drawn for low-vision readers. Overpass is commonly described as derived from Highway Gothic, the US road-sign face, though that lineage was not verified in this research. GOV.UK made a similar choice: its type began as Transport, the British road-sign face ([Web Historian](https://webhistorian.co.uk/how-uk-government-built-govuk-global-template-public-sector-web-design/)). Switching to a serif would not buy trust. A 2008 banking-site study found **no significant trust difference between serif and sans**, and small studies of older readers (20 people) found faster, more comfortable reading in sans on paper and a stated preference for sans on tablets ([PMC review, 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9680897/)). The "editorial" counter-trend of cream backgrounds and serif headlines is itself becoming an AI default ([SaaS Landing Page](https://saaslandingpage.com/articles/5-design-trends-for-saas-and-ai-landing-pages-in-2026/); [Falia](https://falia.co/en/insights/web-design/spot-ai-built-website/)), so adopting it would only restart the clock. A serif does belong on the printed documents, the status letter and the agreement, where it signals that the page is a record.

The fix is restraint applied to the same brand. These rules turn the palette from decoration into meaning:

| Element | Rule | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Color roles | Pine and paper as the base. Road-sign yellow only for "needs your attention" (an amount due, an open vote), the way real signs use it. Brick only for overdue items and errors. Lake blue only for links | Colored edges reserved for status messages ([Paralect](https://www.paralect.com/blog/signs-of-ai-slop-in-design)); color roles are an inference |
| Borders and cards | Sections are borderless by default, separated by whitespace and rules. Borders mark only what must stand apart | Let spacing and headings do the grouping ([Paralect](https://www.paralect.com/blog/signs-of-ai-slop-in-design)); [vibecodekit](https://vibecodekit.dev/ai-slop-design) |
| Corners and shadows | Not one radius and one shadow on everything. Documents get square corners and a paper edge; buttons stay rounded | Uniformity "flattens hierarchy" ([925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-web-design-guide)) |
| Emphasis | One emphasized element per page | "Once per page at most" ([Paralect](https://www.paralect.com/blog/signs-of-ai-slop-in-design)) |
| Labels | No all-caps eyebrow over a heading unless it carries real information, such as a date | [Paralect](https://www.paralect.com/blog/signs-of-ai-slop-in-design) |
| Icons | Inline beside a word, labeling a function. Never centered in a tinted square on top of a card | Tinted icon squares called a "near-universal AI pattern" ([vibe0](https://www.vibe0.com.au/blog/how-to-tell-if-a-website-is-vibe-coded)) |
| Graphics | Real screenshots, real document pages, real photos. No gradients, glow, frosted glass or floating "toast" cards | [Developers Digest](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/ai-design-slop-and-how-to-spot-it); [vibe0](https://www.vibe0.com.au/blog/how-to-tell-if-a-website-is-vibe-coded) |
| Section shapes | Vary them: a paragraph, a table, a full document, a two-column list | Identical section rhythm is a tell ([925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-web-design-guide)) |

Do the people Road Fund serves notice any of this? No study shows that everyday users, let alone rural 60-year-olds, recognize these patterns as AI-made. The evidence comes from designers, developers and two blog-level audits. Research on copy cuts both ways. In Bynder's 2024 survey of 2,000 UK and US adults, about half picked out the ChatGPT article, and 56% of those with a preference liked it better ([Bynder](https://www.bynder.com/en/press-media/ai-vs-human-made-content-study)). Yet 52% said they felt less engaged once told the content was AI-made, a figure reported by the [CIM](https://cim.co.uk/content-hub/editorial/ai-content-creation-balancing-innovation-and-authenticity) and not checked against Bynder's own report. The penalty comes from suspicion. The redesign is worth doing even if no captain ever consciously thinks "AI." In Fogg's study of 2,684 people rating live sites, design look was the most common credibility theme, at **46.1% of comments overall and 54.6% for finance sites** ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/997078.997097)). Road Fund collects money through a link, which puts it in the harshest category. NN/g also watched older adults install ad blockers and skip sponsored results ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-for-senior-citizens/)). That suggests, without proving it, that marketing-flavored decoration triggers the same learned avoidance. Every replacement below, from a real phone number to a real screenshot, has its own evidence as a credibility signal.

## Checkable facts make a small product look established

The core credibility research is old but consistent. Stanford's guidelines call "listing a physical address" the simplest way to show that a real organization stands behind a site, and Guideline 5 asks sites to "show that honest and trustworthy people stand behind" them ([Stanford Web Credibility Guidelines](http://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines/index.html)). In the 2002 survey, a physical address raised credibility more than staff photos did, with means of 1.86 and 1.53, though both helped ([Fogg et al.](https://credibility.stanford.edu/pdf/p61-fogg.pdf)). NN/g's four credibility factors were first listed in 1999 and reconfirmed since: design quality; up-front disclosure of costs; comprehensive, correct and current content; and connection to the rest of the web ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)). Users expect About content to be "clear, authentic, and transparent," and hollow words like "revolutionary" make them distrust a site. They also cross-check claims against outside reviews ([NN/g About Us](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/about-us-summaries/)). Companies that hide their contact details "are seen as evasive and unreliable" ([NN/g Contact Us](https://www-nngroup-com.translate.goog/articles/contact-us-pages/?_x_tr_sl=en&_x_tr_tl=id&_x_tr_hl=id&_x_tr_pto=tc)). Road Fund has none of these identity signals today. The footer in `components/site-chrome.tsx` gives no company name, town, mailing address, phone number or person, and the site has no About page. This is the largest credibility gap on the site. Design work cannot close it. Only the owner's real facts can.

Small products that earned trust without scale used the same moves. HOA Express, a tiny HOA website builder with a free plan, says it has no outside investors and that "our entire team works together in the same office in Houston" ([HOA Express](https://www.hoa-express.com/about)). When it raised prices, it called the change "our first pricing change since our company was founded nearly 13 years ago" and said plainly who would pay more and who would pay less ([HOA Express blog](https://blog.hoa-express.com/announcing-new-pricing/)). Fathom calls itself "customer-funded" with "zero investors" ([Fathom](https://usefathom.com/about)) and promises "We'll email you before your plan starts billing, and you can cancel anytime" ([Fathom pricing](https://usefathom.com/pricing)). A 2026 teardown credited "the founders' brand" with most of its conversion ([Unlock SaaS](https://unlocksaas.com/funnel-teardown/fathom)). Plausible explains that "since we don't make money from advertising, we need to charge a fee" ([Plausible](https://plausible.io/blog/growing-saas-mrr)) and links to a live demo of its real dashboard ([Plausible](https://plausible.io/)). Breeze, church software that volunteers run, prices itself in one sentence: "$72 per month (US Dollars), no set up fees, no termination fees, cancel anytime, unlimited people, and unlimited users" ([The Lead Pastor](https://theleadpastor.com/tools/breeze-church-management-review/)). Carfax puts "View sample report" next to each package ([CARFAX Canada](https://www.carfax.ca/vehicle-history/sample-report)), which is the direct model for selling a $150 status letter. PayHOA leads with scale proof, "6,000+ communities" and 4.7 stars from 1,000+ reviews ([PayHOA](https://www.payhoa.com/)). A new product cannot honestly copy that, and nothing should be put in its place.

Rural electric co-ops are the closest analog to Road Fund's audience, and they lead with their own people and land. Examples include a lineworker in an Oregon landscape ([Powerful, ORECA](https://www.powerfulweb.com/case-study/oregon-rural-electric-cooperative-association/)), a hand-illustrated footer of barns, pasture and rural roads ([Powerful, SWAEC](https://www.powerfulweb.com/case-study/southwest-arkansas-electric-cooperative/)), a board and line crews listed by name ([Glacier Electric](https://www.wix.com/explore/websites/site/glacier-electric-cooperative)), and homepages built around account login and top tasks ([Powerful](https://www.powerfulweb.com/2022/10/10-inspirational-examples-of-great-utility-web-design/)). These come from one web vendor's portfolio, not from an independent sample. GOV.UK shows the plainest route. Its early look was defined by "no photography, no promotional banners, and no attempt to make government seem exciting" ([Web Historian](https://webhistorian.co.uk/how-uk-government-built-govuk-global-template-public-sector-web-design/)), and that plainness became its authority. Craigslist users interviewed after 25 years expressed "a consistent fondness for the website's bare-bones aesthetic" ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/craigslist-turns-25-a-reminder-that-a-more-democratic-version-of-the-internet-can-still-thrive-129875)). An all-type, document-first page is a legitimate style for Road Fund until real photos exist.

People skip decoration and look at real things. Nielsen's 2010 eye-tracking work found that "big feel-good images that are purely decorative" were mostly ignored, while people studied photos of real people at length (reported through [Netimperative](https://netimperative.com/?p=735); the original was not read). In a vendor-published A/B test, Harrington Movers replaced a stock couple with a photo of its own crew, or of its own truck, and quote requests rose **45.45% and 45.05%** at 98% confidence ([VWO](https://vwo.com/blog/stock-photos-reduce-conversions)). That is the closest analog to a local service showing its own road and plow truck. AI imagery adds a new risk. In Getty's survey of 30,000+ adults, 98% called authentic images essential to trust and 78% said an AI image cannot count as "authentic" ([Getty Images](https://newsroom.gettyimages.com/en/getty-images/nearly-90-of-consumers-want-transparency-on-ai-images-finds-getty-images-report)); Getty sells photos, so it has an interest. In a Clutch survey, 57% of consumers could not tell AI photos from real ones ([Clutch](https://clutch.co/resources/ai-photos-brand-usage-consumer-trust)). A 2025 study found that customers see AI images as "lacking veracity," more strongly for high-involvement decisions ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0268401225000866)), though attitudes match when people don't know an image is AI ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3387/15/10/395)). Road Fund asks people over 55 to send money through a link, so an AI road photo that looks slightly off is costly if anyone notices. The rule follows: use real photos of real private roads, captioned with road, county, state and month, taken by the founder or by captains who give permission. Until those exist, use type, real screenshots and real document pages, never stock photos or AI images. Crop screenshots to one benefit; a full screen "communicates complexity, not value" ([Techtippr](https://techtippr.com/saas-landing-page-screenshots/); vendor opinion, not a test). Jobber's rebrand paired real customers' photos with "hand-drawn visual grit" ([Jake Belke](https://jakebelke.com/jobber)). A hand-drawn, plat-style map of the sample road would give Road Fund a signature graphic that no template ships with.

No designer or model can write any of the items in the next table. They have to come from the owner. Until a real fact exists, the page should leave its slot empty rather than fill it with a placeholder. The demo road's people and places (Carol Jensen, Birch Hollow Lane) are fictional. They belong only on pages clearly labeled "Sample road," never presented as customers.

| What the owner must supply | Where it goes | Until it exists |
|---|---|---|
| Founder's real name, a real photo, town and state, and two or three sentences on why they built it (ideally tied to their own road) | "Who makes Road Fund" block on the homepage; an About page | Leave the block out. No stock or AI portrait, no invented bio |
| Legal company name and state of registration | Footer, terms, receipts, card statement descriptor | Needed before taking real money |
| Mailing address | Footer and Contact page | A P.O. box is fine if the page says so |
| A phone or text number answered by a person, with hours | Site header and footer, captain Help, the Stripe step, the first-bill step | A "Request a call back" form with a stated reply time |
| Real photos of real private roads (gravel, a plow mid-pass, a mailbox cluster, a culvert), with permission and captions | Hero and section breaks | Type, screenshots and document pages only |
| Real captain quotes with first name, road, town and state, with written permission | Near pricing | Nothing at all: no placeholder quotes, logo rows, user counts or star ratings |
| Current screenshots of the real product | Hero; "What your neighbors see" | Screenshots of the demo road, labeled "Sample road" |
| How Road Fund makes money, and where neighbors' money goes | A short "How Road Fund makes money" box and the FAQ | Must match the build: plan fees, $1 per online payment, the $150 letter fee, and money going through Stripe to the bank account the captain connects |
| Billing promises such as "We'll email you before you're charged" | Pricing page | State only what the billing system actually does |
| A dated changelog and "Updated" dates | Footer, pricing, road law guide | Start with the redesign date |

Assembled from these peers, the homepage would run in this order. A plain header with How it works, Pricing, Selling a home, Help, Sign in and a visible phone number. A left-aligned hero that names the job, beside a real screenshot. A worked bill for a labeled sample road. "What your neighbors see," showing the actual text and pay page. The agreement and the sample status letter shown as real pages. A price table with every fee. "Who makes Road Fund," including how it makes money. A plain FAQ. A footer with contact details, terms, a changelog and a last-updated date. Each element comes from a peer, but none has been tested for conversion at Road Fund's size.

## Concise, objective copy tested 124% more usable than marketese

Morkes and Nielsen's 1997 study is still the best-known evidence on marketing copy. Fifty-one users worked with five versions of the same site's text. The concise version tested **58% more usable**, the scannable version 47%, and the version that was concise, scannable and objective all at once tested **124% more usable**. Users "detested 'marketese'" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/applying-writing-guidelines-web-pages/); [Nielsen Alertbox archive](https://chnm.gmu.edu/digitalhistory/links/pdf/chapter4/4.17c.pdf)). Only one source gives the figure for objective language alone, about 27%. People also judge precise numbers more believable than round ones, because sharp figures seem less likely to be estimates ([Improbable Research](https://improbable.com/2016/08/04/round-numbers-sharp-numbers-and-their-perceived-credibility/)). The audience makes the case stronger. In 2023, **28% of US adults scored at Level 1 or below in literacy**, up from 19% in 2017 ([NCES PIAAC](https://nces.ed.gov/surveys/piaac/2023/national_results.asp)). GOV.UK splits any sentence over 25 words, prefers "buy" to "purchase," and avoids negative contractions because readers misread them ([GOV.UK](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/)). Councils that follow it aim for a reading age of 9, and Surrey keeps sentences under 20 words ([Surrey County Council](https://www.surreycc.gov.uk/website/writing-guide/reading-levels-for-web-pages)). The CDC's Clear Communication Index asks for one main message, at the top, in active voice, using "words the primary audience uses" ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/ccindex/pdf/clear-communication-user-guide.pdf)). Service names should "describe a task, not a technology," be verbs, and avoid "portal," "hub" and "dashboard" ([DfE](https://design.education.gov.uk/content-design/naming-service)). Many seniors are unsure what "page," "homepage" or "website" mean ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/define-techy-words-old-users/)).

The AI tells in writing come more from sentence structure than from vocabulary. Wikipedia's editor guide to signs of AI writing lists words to watch (boasts, vibrant, showcase, delve, pivotal), "negative parallelisms" such as "Not just X, but also Y," overuse of the rule of three, and a drift toward "advertisement-like writing." It has moved em dashes into "historical indicators," because surface tells fade once readers learn them ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)). Word lists go out of date fast. A study of more than 15 million PubMed abstracts found "delves" 28 times and "underscores" 13.8 times more frequent in 2024 than expected, and estimated that at least 13.5% of 2024 abstracts had passed through an LLM ([Science Advances](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adt3813); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.07016)). Editors already report "delve" fading. Writers are dropping "It's not just X, it's Y" because readers now treat it as a warning sign ([Slate](https://slate.com/technology/2025/08/chatgpt-artificial-intelligence-shaming-paranoia-writing.html)). In SaaS copy, "seamless" usually stands in for a feature nobody described, and "No X. No Y. Just Z." gets repeated section after section ([Olivia Cal](https://www.oliviacal.com/post/ai-writing-tells)). Road Fund's copy already avoids most of the listed words. What remains is the shape: "The same **three** problems" sits above three cards, which sit above three steps. "Priced for a road, **not** an HOA" is a negative parallelism. "**Built so** your neighbors trust it" and the footer's "Built for the neighbor who keeps the road running" are self-descriptions. Several headings are slogans ending in periods, and the page closes on a "without the chasing" kicker. A quick check helps: if PayHOA's name could replace Road Fund's in a line, the line is too generic ([Growthguys](https://growthguys.tech/blog/genuine-website-vs-ai-slop.html)).

The best model is the bill-pay pages rural people already use. Co-op pages label their guest path "Pay Now (no login required)" and "Quick Pay – No Login Required." They spell out exactly what the payer needs, "the LAST NAME of the primary account holder … and the billing ACCOUNT NUMBER" ([Craighead Electric](https://craigheadelectric.coop/smarthub-101-pay-now-without-registering/)), and they say what the guest path cannot do: "you cannot view your usage, report an outage or pay multiple bill types at once" ([NineStar](https://www.ninestarconnect.com/account/)). Counties publish their fees in plain words: "Electronic Check: No fee!", and cards at "2.29% of the tax amount with a $1.00 minimum" ([Larimer County](https://www.larimer.gov/treasurer/pay/creditcard)). The IRS describes Direct Pay as one-time payments "without signing in or paying fees" ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-reminder-direct-pay-offers-free-easy-way-to-pay-taxes)). Square's emails say "Pay Invoice," and sellers complain when an email client shows it as text rather than a real button ([Square Support](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5138-pay-invoices-with-square); [Square Community](https://community.squareup.com/t5/Feature-Requests/Create-a-box-around-quot-Pay-Invoice-quot-in-the-customer-s/idi-p/847129)). For Road Fund, that means the button names the action and the amount, a short note says what it costs and what the payer needs, and the page states its own limits. "No login required" is already familiar to anyone who pays an electric co-op bill online. 37signals summed up the principle as "Copywriting is interface design" ([Signal v. Noise](https://signalvnoise.com/archives2/getting_real_copywriting_is_interface_design)).

The earlier strategy round said the headline "Stop fronting the money for your road" captured the right position. The position holds. The wording should change, because an imperative slogan with a period is the shape this round's evidence flags. The rewrites below use example figures that must be replaced with Road Fund's real prices and the owner's real details.

| Now | Rewrite | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Stop fronting the money for your road." | "Split the plowing and gravel bill with everyone on your road" (no closing period). Subhead: "Each house gets its share and the reason by text, email or letter. They pay by bank, card or check, and you see who has paid. Setup takes about 10 minutes." | States the job in captains' words and says what happens next (CDC: main message first) |
| "The same three problems come up on almost every private road" | "What usually goes wrong on a private road," followed by the existing line "Someone pays the plow driver in December and the grader in May…" as a paragraph | Drops the announced three. That line is already good voice-of-customer copy |
| "How it works: Agree on a fair split / Ask once, clearly / Everyone sees the same numbers" | A worked example: "2026 plowing, Northern Plow, $1,260 split 9 ways = $140 a house. Each house gets a text from you with that line and a link. They pay online or mail you a check. Everyone sees the same list of who has paid." | Nouns and numbers in place of three abstract fragments |
| "Put the deal in writing" | "Road maintenance agreement" | Uses the noun buyers, lenders and title companies use |
| "Ready when a house on the road sells" | "When a house on your road sells" | Names the situation people search for |
| "Built so your neighbors trust it" | "What your neighbors see," showing the real text and pay page | Shows the evidence instead of claiming the result |
| "Priced for a road, not an HOA" | "What it costs," followed by a table | Removes the negative parallelism and lets the exact prices persuade |
| "Get this year's road money in without the chasing" | Remove; end with "Set up your road" and "Questions? Call or text [owner's number]" | Avoids repeating the hero and puts a person last |

|---|---|
| Get started | Set up your road |
| Continue (in setup) | Save 7 homes · Use equal shares · Preview the bill · Send to 7 neighbors |
| Pay now | Pay $140.00 from my checking account |
| Learn more | See a sample road |
| Invalid phone number | Enter a 10-digit phone number, like 231-555-0142 |

A few smaller habits finish the job. Headings use sentence case with no closing period, and em dashes stay rare. Mine real captains' own words for headings, which Joanna Wiebe describes as stealing the message "directly from your prospects" ([Copyhackers](https://copyhackers.com/2014/10/amazon-review-mining/)). Candidate phrases such as "plow guy," "road money" and "the neighbor who won't pay" were not researched and should be checked against real forum posts before use.

## Captains need a to-do list, big labeled buttons and a person to call

The usability gap for older users is large and starts early. In NN/g's 2013 study, users 65+ were **43% slower** than users 21–55. Their task success was **55.3% against 74.5%**, and they were **almost twice as likely to give up** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-seniors-improvements/)). NN/g has since marked that article outdated, but its 2019 follow-up still lists tiny and light-colored type, small targets, inflexible interfaces and gestures as problems, and tells designers to "assign the blame to bad design" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-for-senior-citizens/)). The slowdown begins well before retirement: task time rises about **0.8% a year from age 25 to 60** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/middle-aged-web-users/)). A 2023 systematic review distilled 27 guidelines for older adults and ranked two above the rest: **"Simplify"** and **"Increase the size and distance between interactive controls."** It also called for fewer alternative paths and text labels on every icon ([JMIR mHealth](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2023/1/e43186)). In a 2025 study of about 1,200 Iranian mobile-banking users aged 60–75, the most severe problems were error prevention and visibility of system status. Missing "last updated" timestamps made users doubt their balances, and usability scores fell from 54.2 at ages 60–63 to 45.3 at 72–75 ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13402424/)). That sample is not American. No rigorous research exists at all on volunteer administrators such as HOA board members or church treasurers, which is a real evidence gap. What is known is that captains do most jobs once a year, so each job has to work for someone who remembers nothing from last time. GOV.UK designs for exactly that, services "used infrequently, by people with a broad range of digital skills and confidence" ([GOV.UK service manual archive](https://github.com/alphagov/government-service-design-manual/blob/master/service-manual/user-centred-design/resources/patterns/form-structure.md)).

Navigation has to stay visible. In NN/g's study of 179 users, hidden navigation was less discoverable than visible navigation on both phones and desktops ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/find-navigation-mobile-even-hamburger/)). A secondary summary reports that mobile users used navigation in **57% of tasks when it sat behind a hamburger, against 86% when part of it was visible**, and were 15% slower ([Smart Insights](https://www.smartinsights.com/digital-marketing-strategy/are-hamburger-menus-hidden-navigation-good-for-ux/)). Dropdowns used as navigation cost users their sense of overview ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-dropdown-navigation)). The captain app (`components/app/shell.tsx`) already uses a visible bottom bar of five labeled tabs, which is the right pattern at the upper limit. Three details undercut it. The labels are 13.5px. Settings and "See neighbor view" are icon-only buttons in the phone header, at about 43px. Help appears only in the desktop sidebar, so a captain on a phone cannot reach it. The rules: at most five tabs, each with a word of at least 16px; the active tab marked by bold text and a bar, not color alone; Help with a phone number visible on every screen; every core job within two taps of Home; and sub-pages no more than one level deep.

Home should open on a to-do list. GOV.UK's task-list research found that users "mainly cared about which tasks they had completed" and "would not start a task unless they could finish it." The 2023 revision made the whole row clickable and sharpened the status tags ([GDS](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2023/12/15/working-as-a-community-to-iterate-the-task-list-pattern/)). Road Fund's home screen (`app/app/page.tsx`) gets halfway there. It shows one big "Next up" action, but it folds the remaining to-dos into a collapsed "N more to do" accordion. It then stacks a seven-item, dismissible "Getting started" checklist, a "Road account" card with "Balance," "Available now" and "still clearing," and an activity log. Checklist benchmarks argue for fewer items. Across 188 companies, average checklist completion was **19.2% and the median 10.1%** ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/?p=197841)), so nothing essential to collecting money should depend on one. Practitioner guidance puts the ceiling at three to five items in a panel that stays put rather than one that can be dismissed ([Kompassify](https://kompassify.com/blog/onboarding-wizard-guide)). The home screen should open with one status sentence, such as "Plowing bill: 5 of 7 homes paid · $1,700 of $2,380 collected · due Dec 15." Below it, one to three to-dos should sit in full view ("2 checks are in the mail. Mark them when they arrive."). Then comes the household list, with a large status for each home: **Paid** (with method and date), **Check in the mail** ("Mary says she mailed it Dec 3"), or **Not yet** (with the last reminder date). Each status gets a word and a color. A short "This year's road jobs" list follows, with Done, Started or Not yet on each row. The balance moves to Money with a "Last updated" time, and the activity log goes below everything else. Each empty section gets one sentence and one button. On Votes, for example: "No votes yet. When the road needs to decide something, like a gravel delivery, start a vote here."

Multi-step jobs should be one question per page and end on a review page. GOV.UK starts every form at "one thing per page" because it helps low-confidence users, works on phones and makes errors and saved progress easier ([GDS](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2015/07/03/one-thing-per-page/); [GOV.UK Design System](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/)). It dropped accordion-style forms after user research ([GOV.UK user research](https://userresearch.blog.gov.uk/2015/08/13/no-more-accordions-how-to-choose-a-form-structure/)). A study of Norwegian public web services and a set of design principles for older adults with mild cognitive impairment both recommend letting users review and approve an action before it is confirmed ([arXiv, Norway](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12552); [arXiv, MCI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00480)). Before anything that cannot be undone, such as sending bills or reminders, closing a vote or issuing a letter, Road Fund should show a full page: "Check before you send: 12 homes will get a text and an email asking for $250 by June 1." That page has one button, "Send bills to 12 homes," and a "Go back and change" link. Recording a check is the exception. It repeats often and fast, which is where one-thing-per-page fits poorly ([Chris Armstrong](https://chrisarmstrong.io/Designing-Internal-Services/)). It should take two taps from a household row: tap the home, then tap "Got their check," with the amount and today's date filled in. Rows for homes on which the neighbor has pressed "I mailed my check" should read "Mary mailed a check Dec 3. Did it arrive? [Yes, got it] [Not yet]," following TurboTenant and TeamSnap, where offline payments are logged per person ([TurboTenant](https://support.turbotenant.com/en/articles/4004079-how-to-record-an-offline-payment); [TeamSnap](https://helpme.teamsnap.com/article/1268-set-up-and-manage-classic-payments)). Edits should offer undo rather than a confirmation dialog: "Check for $250 from Smith saved. Undo," shown for at least ten seconds. Errors should say what went wrong and how to fix it ([GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/error-message)). That matters because Baymard found **98% of sites use generic error messages** and some test users abandoned solely because of them ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/adaptive-validation-error-messages)). Validate a field only once the user has finished it, and clear the error as soon as it is fixed ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/inline-form-validation)). In a month-long study, older adults' error rates did not improve with familiarity, and the system started the recovery in **under 5%** of errors ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.02421v3)). The interface has to do the explaining. WCAG 2.2 adds three requirements: no time limits that can't be extended, information already given is never asked for twice, and sign-in never depends on remembering a password or solving a puzzle ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/timing-adjustable.html); [Deque](https://dequeuniversity.com/resources/wcag-2.2)). Road Fund's emailed sign-in link already meets the last of these.

Setup should be a short wizard that ends with the first bill. NN/g says wizards suit infrequent, unfamiliar jobs. A wizard should show every step with the current one highlighted, keep the order fixed, use descriptive button labels instead of "Next," and let users save and come back ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/wizards/)). The current wizard (`components/setup/wizard.tsx`) gets several things right: "Step 3 of 6," "About 4 min left," and "Save and exit." It marks progress with six thin colored bars, though, and every step's button says "Continue." On phones, the Back button shows only an arrow. Connecting the bank is step 4, even though in live mode it is already postponed until after setup. The bank step should come out of the wizard and become the first checklist item after the first bill. That bill goes out with "mail a check" working from day one. Stripe's identity check is the riskiest step for a volunteer. Since 2024, Stripe asks US account holders for their date of birth and the **last four digits of their Social Security number** ([Stripe Support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/2024-updates-to-us-verification-requirements-faq); [Stripe Support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/why-do-you-need-my-date-of-birth-and-4-digits-of-my-social-security-number)). Stripe's own article cites an unsourced claim that some platforms lose "up to 70%" of users at identity verification ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/resources/more/crypto-onboarding-best-practices)). Before the handoff, Road Fund should say what is coming: "To send money to the road's bank account, our payment company, Stripe, has to confirm who you are. It will ask for your date of birth and the last 4 digits of your Social Security number. Road Fund never sees or keeps them. Have your bank's account and routing numbers ready. It takes about 10 minutes." The ten-minute figure is an unverified estimate. Add "Rather do this together on the phone? Call us" only if someone will answer. The same applies to PayHOA's "send us your file and we'll import it" service ([PayHOA Help](https://intercom.help/payhoa/en/articles/3159366-importer-video)). Its rural version, "Send us a photo of your paper list and we'll type it in," helps only if the owner staffs it.

| Element | Rule | Road Fund today | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body text | 18–19px on every screen size. Nothing that matters below 16px. Headings 24px or more | 18px root (good). Tab labels 13.5px; status chips, eyebrows and captions about 15px | GOV.UK body text is 19px and no longer drops to 16px on phones ([GOV.UK issue](https://github.com/alphagov/govuk-design-system/issues/2289)) |
| Contrast | 7:1 or better for body text, 4.5:1 for secondary text, 3:1 for input and button borders | Charcoal on paper is 14.5:1, but many paragraphs use the gray "stone" at 5.8:1 | W3C chose 4.5:1 partly to offset reduced contrast sensitivity with age ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum)) |
| Tap targets | 48px or more for every control. Primary buttons full width and 56–64px tall. 8–12px between targets | Large buttons 63px and medium 54px (good). Small buttons 45px; header icon buttons about 43px | WCAG AAA is 44px ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). Older adults' touches landed mostly within 8mm, roughly 50 CSS px ([Kobayashi et al.](https://savie.ca/BaseConnaissances/upload/pdf/3034_kobayashietal_2011_elderlyuser_evaluation_of_mobile_touchscreen_Index3034.pdf)), and bigger, wider-spaced buttons helped ([Hwangbo et al.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4967054/)) |

| Feedback | Banners stay at least 10 seconds or until the next action. "Saved" after each step | Saving indicator appears; undo banners missing | Inference from the 43% slowdown |

The millimeter-to-pixel conversion is approximate (about 0.16mm per CSS pixel on current phones). Studies on large screens found accuracy best around 14–17mm ([Jin et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/225367546_Touch_Screen_User_Interfaces_for_Older_Adults_Button_Size_and_Spacing)), but those results may not transfer to phones. That is why 48px is set as the floor rather than the goal.

## The pay page has to read as the opposite of a scam text

Road Fund's main channel looks like the most common fraud. People reported losing **$470 million to scams that began with a text in 2024**, five times the 2020 figure. The leading types included fake package fees, fake bank alerts and fake unpaid-toll texts that link to a payment page ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/04/new-ftc-data-show-top-text-message-scams-2024-overall-losses-text-scams-hit-470-million); [FTC Data Spotlight](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/data-visualizations/data-spotlight/2025/04/top-text-scams-2024)). Reported text-scam losses among older adults rose **61% in one year**, according to secondary coverage of the FTC's 2026 report to Congress ([AccountsRecovery.net](https://www.accountsrecovery.net/2026/10/22/ftc-older-adults-reported-3-billion-in-fraud-losses-in-2025/)). The FTC tells people not to tap links in unexpected texts and to check with the sender "using a phone number or website you know is real" ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-and-report-spam-text-messages)). AARP names urgency as the core trick and treats specificity as a sign a message is real: fake recall notices "usually don't mention a specific product" ([AARP](https://www.aarp.org/money/scams-fraud/fake-recall-notices/)). Scammers now ask iPhone users to "reply Y" so that blocked links become tappable ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/2575750/hackers-have-devised-a-simple-text-scam-to-bypass-apples-iphone-protections.html)). Carriers block public link shorteners such as bit.ly and expect a branded link domain ([Microsoft Azure](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-my/azure/communication-services/concepts/sms/sms-faq); [Bird](https://bird.com/explained/sms/why-do-carriers-block-public-link-shorteners)). Legitimate brands also publish what they will never do. AARP, for example, "will never ask for money, credit card details, or personal information via text" ([AARP info sheet](https://tvfcu.com/_/kcms-doc/1344/88862/AARP-Is-It-a-Scam-Info-Sheet.pdf)). Road Fund's texts (`lib/templates.ts`) already follow most of this. They name the captain in full and the road, state the amount, what it is for and the date, add a "Not sure about this? Call me at…" line, and use no exclamation marks. The weak spot is the link, which is the site's origin plus `/n/road_…/` and a 22-character random token. A shorter link on Road Fund's own domain, the same domain every time, would read better in a text while remaining unguessable, for example a ten-character code with rate limits; that suggestion is an engineering inference. Road Fund should never use a public shortener and never ask anyone to reply to activate a link.

The top of the pay page should answer the neighbor's four questions before anything else. Square, co-op Pay Now pages and county pay pages converge on the same order: who is asking, how much, for what and by when, and one action ([Square Support](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5138-pay-invoices-with-square); [Craighead Electric](https://craigheadelectric.coop/smarthub-101-pay-now-without-registering/)). Today the neighbor page (`components/neighbor/neighbor-app.tsx`) opens on the road name as an all-caps eyebrow, then "Hi Hank" and a lot number. The captain's name and phone sit in a "Questions?" card at the bottom of the page. "Why this amount?" is a good feature, but it hides the whole explanation behind a tap. The order below moves the person and the reason to the top. Its names and number come from the labeled demo road.

| Order | What shows | Example (sample road) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who sent it | "From Carol Jensen, road captain, Birch Hollow Lane" |
| 2 | The amount, as the largest text on the page | "$140.00" |
| 3 | What it is for and when it is due | "Your share of winter plowing · due Dec 15" |
| 4 | One line of reason, always visible, with details behind a link | "Northern Plow, $1,260 split 9 ways. See how it was split." |
| 5 | A way to check without the link | "Not sure this is real? Call Carol at (231) 555-0113." |
| 6 | Three equal choices as large radio cards | "From my checking account: no fee" / "Debit or credit card," with its fee in dollars if the road lawfully passes one on / "Mail a check: no fee" |
| 7 | A shaded, bordered payment box with the total and one button | "Pay $140.00 from my checking account" |
| 8 | One plain reassurance line | "Payments are handled by Stripe. Road Fund will never text you for a card number, bank password or Social Security number." |

Fees belong on the options, in dollars, before any payment details. Baymard found that dropdowns for choosing a payment method perform poorly while radio-style choices test well ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/payment-method-selection)). The full total, including fees, should be visible before users are asked for payment details, because a cost that first shows up at the payment step, "however small, triggers reconsideration" ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/reduce-cart-abandonment)). In secondary summaries of Baymard's 2025 survey, extra costs were the top reason for abandoning checkout at 39%, ahead of distrust of the site with card details and forced account creation at 19% each. Older rounds put extra costs at 48% ([Red Stag summary](https://redstagfulfillment.com/percentage-of-online-shoppers-abandon-their-cart/); [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-ux)). Counties list e-check first as free and name the processor that keeps the card fee: "Any convenience fee paid does not go to the County" ([Madera County](https://www.maderacounty.com/government/treasurer-tax-collector/pay-property-taxes/pay-by-credit-card)). Stripe charges 2.9% + 30¢ on cards and 0.8% capped at $5 on bank payments ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/pricing)). On a $340 share, that is about **$10.16 by card against $2.72 by bank**, before Road Fund's $1. Instant bank verification is billed separately at $1.50 per verification, which Road Fund's own pricing needs to absorb. A bank payment costs the road so little that the road can reasonably cover it, which lets the bank option honestly say "No fee," the way counties treat e-checks. The examples in this section assume that setup. If a card fee is passed on, it appears on the option in dollars (on a $140 share, Stripe's 2.9% + 30¢ plus Road Fund's $1 comes to $5.36), subject to the surcharge limits below. QuickBooks shows what a surprise fee does: owners protested a $25 bank-payment fee that customers saw "Not approved by me" ([QuickBooks Community](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/community/payments-3/customer-was-charged-a-25-convenience-fee-for-online-payment-new-feature-qb-added-and-turned-on-not-approved-by-me-anyone-able-to-get-this-reversed-20092)).

This round's evidence changes one earlier decision. The pay page currently pre-selects nothing, a rule the earlier strategy round set because 56% of people in a 2024 survey named security as their top reason not to pay by bank ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/pay-by-bank-and-the-merchant-payments-use-case-benefits-20250707.html)). County and co-op pages, which rural payers already use, lead with the free bank option. So the bank option should be listed first, labeled "No fee," and pre-selected, with card and check shown at the same size directly below it. One sentence should address the distrust: "Comes out of your checking account once, like a check." Watch the first real payments. If neighbors abandon at the bank step, drop the pre-selection.

Two payment details carry real risk. First, the bank path should use **instant verification only**. Stripe's fallback, manual account entry with microdeposits, makes the payer wait 1–2 business days, find a deposit coded "SM" on a statement, and confirm it through an emailed link within 23 hours ([Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/ach-direct-debit/accept-a-payment)). An older neighbor is unlikely to come back for that second visit, and it looks just like the "verify your account" emails scammers send. The current copy offers "or type your account and routing numbers," and that path should go. Anyone without online banking can mail a check. Second, passing card fees to payers is legally fragile. The road setting `payerCoversFees` adds the card fee to every card payment, debit included. According to law-firm and vendor summaries, card networks forbid surcharging debit and prepaid cards, Visa caps credit surcharges at 3%, and Connecticut, Massachusetts and Maine ban or heavily restrict them ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/fin-tech/1604562/want-to-have-customers-cover-payment-processing-fees-what-you-need-to-know-about-credit-card-surcharging); [GoDaddy](https://www.godaddy.com/help/credit-surcharging-guide-for-merchants-41822)). There are three safer setups. The road can absorb card fees. The bank option can be framed as a discount ("Pay by bank and save $10"). Or the fee can be an explained, optional "cover the card fee" choice that is never pre-checked, as Tithe.ly offers donors ([Tithe.ly](https://support.tithe.ly/hc/en-us/articles/32266860374679--Cover-the-Fees-Explained)). Counsel should confirm the choice before launch.

The payment area and the confirmation each need distinct visuals. Baymard found that users judge security by feel. Borders, shading and short reassuring text around the payment fields make that part of a form seem safer, provided the styling is unique to those fields ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/perceived-security-of-payment-form); [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/visually-reinforce-sensitive-fields)). Many shoppers have no preference among security seals ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/site-seal-trust)), so the processor's name and a plain line do more than badges. When the review step and the confirmation look alike, up to 10% of mobile shoppers abandon ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/mcommerce-usability/benchmark/mobile-page-types/review-order)), and people often wait for an email before believing they are done ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/order-confirmation-page)). Road Fund's confirmation already shows a check mark, the amount and a receipt number. It should add "Carol has been told," a "Print or save this receipt" button, and "On its way, clears by Dec 6" for bank payments. The interim screen should say "We have your payment. It will show here in a few minutes" in place of "Payment received by Stripe." Errors should give the neighbor three ways out: "Your bank didn't accept this account. Try another account, pay by card, or mail a check."

Paper and slow connections are part of the design. No product reviewed lets a payer report a mailed check. TurboTenant tells tenants who paid outside the platform to ask the landlord to log it, and until then they keep getting reminders ([TurboTenant](https://support.turbotenant.com/en/articles/8243030-i-paid-my-landlord-outside-of-turbotenant-and-i-am-still-getting-notifications)). Zoho users have asked for exactly this control ([Zoho Community](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/community/topic/eft-direct-posit-recording-in-portal)). Road Fund's "I mailed my check" button, which stops reminders, is a real advantage and should stay. Checks fit this kind of payment. More than half of consumers write at least one a year, use rises with age, and checks are common for paying contractors ([Atlanta Fed](https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/publications/policy-hub-macroblog/2026/10/08/surveying-consumers-use-of-personal-checks)). A 2020 industry white paper reports that **more than half of lockbox checks arrive without their payment coupon** ([CAMS](https://www.camsmgt.com/pdf/Best%20Practices%20for%20HOA%20Payment%20Solutions.pdf)). Every bill should therefore have a printable one-page version with the payee, the mailing address, the exact memo-line text ("Birch Hollow – Lot 6"), a tear-off stub and the short code. The connection matters too. In Pew's 2025 survey, **71% of rural adults had home broadband against 84% in suburbs, and about 20% of rural adults were online only by phone** ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/01/08/internet-use-smartphone-ownership-digital-divides-in-u-s/); [Pew fact sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/internet-broadband/)). Pew labels these figures inconsistently across its pages, so treat them as approximate. Today the neighbor page renders in the browser and shows "Opening your page…" before anything else. The amount, the sender and the Pay button should arrive in the first server response, in a page small enough for a weak cell signal (a target of roughly 200 KB, an inference), with no map or image above the amount.

## Fix identity, the hero and the pay page first

Several things are already right and should survive the redesign. The neighbor texts name the captain in full and give a way to check without the link. The pay page needs no account, shows fees before the button, offers check as an equal choice, has "I mailed my check," and uses "Take $X from my checking account once." Buttons are large, the root text size is 18px, and the setup wizard shows the step number, the minutes left and "Save and exit." Craigslist and NN/g both warn against moving things that people have learned ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/craigslist-turns-25-a-reminder-that-a-more-democratic-version-of-the-internet-can-still-thrive-129875); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-seniors-improvements/)), so the changes below should ship together, once, and then hold still. Priority 1 means before the next real captain or neighbor uses the product. Priority 2 means the following release. Priority 3 means when time allows.

The redesign should be tested before it ships, and with the right people. NN/g's numbers come from users 65+. The planned changes should be tried with five to eight captains aged 65 to 75, plus a few neighbors who get a real-looking text, timing three jobs: send the first bill, record a check, and pay a share by check and by bank. The relevant baselines are roughly 55% task success and twice the give-up rate. Recruit from the older end, since usability scores in the banking study dropped about nine points between the early 60s and the early 70s.

## Conclusion

This redesign is mostly a sourcing job. The tells that make Road Fund look generated are defaults that nobody chose. The signals that make a product look established are facts a template cannot produce: a founder's name and town, a phone that rings, a road someone photographed in January, a fee printed to the cent, a sample letter in full. Style trends turn over quickly, and today's cream-and-serif fix is already on its way to becoming the next tell. Specifics do not age that way. The practical consequence is a staffing decision for the owner: an About section, a phone line and a photo library are product work, and the redesign should not launch with placeholder versions of them.

Road Fund also has two audiences who trust different things. Captains decide whether to trust Road Fund, so the marketing site has to show the company. Neighbors decide whether to trust the captain, so every neighbor-facing surface, from the text and the pay page to the receipt and the paper slip, should show the captain's name and phone number first and Road Fund's brand second. The weakest evidence in this report is also the most important: nobody has studied volunteer road captains or how rural 70-year-olds judge a payment link. A one-week test with a handful of real captains would do more to settle the open questions, such as the bank default, the wizard order and the size of the home screen, than any further desk research.
